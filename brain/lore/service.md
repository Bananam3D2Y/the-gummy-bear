# How service works at The Gummy Bear

Every ticket that comes in names one dish. A ticket that waits too long becomes a walkout, and a walkout is worse than a late plate: the table leaves, the tip leaves with them, and the rail stays just as long.

Two portions of prep make one dish. Prep comes out of the walk-in, and the walk-in only holds ten portions. A delivery restocks it slowly, so pulling prep faster than you cook it leaves the next person with nothing.

Finished dishes only go out from the pass. Serving a ticket on time earns a full tip. Serving it late earns almost nothing, but it is still better than a walkout.

The oldest ticket is almost always the right one to work. Cooking whatever is easiest leaves the old tickets to expire while the rail fills behind them.

When three or more tickets are open, stop starting new things and clear the rail. A kitchen that works in order stays ahead; a kitchen that works on whatever is interesting falls behind.

If you are holding a dish nobody has ordered, someone cooked the wrong thing. Call it out so the next one is right.
